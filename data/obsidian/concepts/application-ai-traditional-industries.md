# Application AI for Traditional Industries

## Summary

The core thesis of Roosh Ventures Fund II. The argument: foundation models (OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral) have created an AI capability layer, but large traditional enterprises — in finance, healthcare, enterprise software, logistics — cannot self-implement AI at scale. 95% of GenAI pilots at big companies fail (MIT/Forbes data). The gap between AI capability and enterprise adoption is a massive opportunity for application-layer startups that build the implementation bridge.

Roosh's target: AI-native startups automating specific high-value workflows within traditional industries, where up to 80% of workflows can theoretically be automated. These companies become the "picks and shovels" for enterprise AI adoption — and potentially the dominant software vendors of the next decade (as ElevenLabs, Kobalt Labs, Rollstack are demonstrating).

The market backdrop from the fundraising deck:
- AI-native startups reaching $100M ARR within 1 year (Lovable, Cursor vs. Shopify taking 6 years)
- European AI ecosystem producing world-class companies (ElevenLabs, DeepMind, Mistral AI) at lower valuations than US peers
- Enterprise AI pilot failure rate of 95% (MIT) creates demand for specialists

## Roosh Ventures Angle

This is not just a thesis — it is the core identity of Fund II. Roosh positions itself as:
1. Better at identifying these companies than generalist VCs (founder pattern-matching)
2. Better at supporting them (Neurons Lab for AI development, Reface for mobile scaling)
3. Better at sourcing them (AI signals via Evertrace, 70+ VC deal-sharing network)

The selection filter: TAM >$1B, founders who are "do-ers" with market insight, companies where AI is the core product (not a feature).

## Contradictions & Open Questions

- Vertical AI vs. Horizontal AI: the fund's portfolio spans both (ElevenLabs is horizontal, Kobalt is vertical). How does the fund decide which to back? [not explicit in current sources]
- "Traditional industries" is broad — the fund doesn't seem to exclude gaming (Zibra), creator economy (Alter), or developer tools (Tower.dev). Where exactly are the boundaries?
